# The Mathematics of Survival: How to Plan a 400 m² Garden to Ensure Full Caloric Self-Sufficiency for a Family

## Abstract

Modern humans do not understand their own metabolism. They regard food as a logistical product delivered by truck. Yet every family functions like a biological furnace burning between 2 and 3 million kilocalories per year. If you cannot produce this energy yourself, you are dependent on a system you do not control.

400 m² of land does not forgive mistakes. There is no room for decorative flowerbeds, trendy microgreens, or plants of low energy value. Every meter must work like a survival mechanism.

This report presents a practical model of a garden capable of providing a family with basic caloric self-sufficiency for 12 months—taking into account soil biology, photosynthetic efficiency, the energy density of crops, and methods of storage without access to industrial refrigeration.

# 1. Introduction

## Hook: The Greatest Lie of Modernity

Modern cities exist thanks to food reserves often measured in days, not months. The supermarket does not produce food. It is merely a transshipment terminal dependent on fuel, synthetic fertilizers, refrigeration, and stable infrastructure.

A person who cannot produce their own calories is exactly three failed deliveries away from starvation.

A 400 m² lawn consumes water, fertilizers, and time, producing zero calories. The same area planted with high-energy-density crops can generate over 1.5 million kcal per year. That is the difference between dependence and biological autonomy.
