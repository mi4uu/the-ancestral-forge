## 2. Background

### The Lost History

The term “adaptogen” was formalized in 1947 by Soviet toxicologist Nikolai Lazarev. His definition was precise: an adaptogen is a substance that (a) is non-toxic in therapeutic doses, (b) produces a non-specific resistance to physical, chemical, or biological stressors, and (c) normalizes physiological functions regardless of the direction of the pathological disturbance. The goal was not “wellness.” The goal was to maximize the combat effectiveness of the Red Army under conditions of extreme cold, hypoxia, and caloric restriction.

Soviet research on *Rhodiola rosea* was conducted at the Central Military Institute of Aviation Medicine in Moscow. The lead researcher, Dr. I.I. Brekhman, published over 200 classified papers between 1950 and 1970. The key findings: *Rhodiola rosea* extract increased the duration of exhaustive swimming in rats by 2.3 times, reduced the time to recover heart rate after maximal exertion by 34%, and improved mental arithmetic performance in sleep-deprived pilots by 18%. The active compounds were identified as rosavin (C₂₀H₂₈O₁₀) and salidroside (C₁₄H₂₀O₇). The ratio of rosavins to salidroside in wild populations ranged from 2.5:1 to 3.5:1, depending on altitude and soil pH.

At the same time, the Chinese military examined *Schisandra chinensis* — known as *wu wei zi* (five-flavor fruit) — and found that a daily dose of 3–6 g of dried berries increased the time to exhaustion in soldiers marching at 4,000 m elevation by 17%, with a corresponding reduction in blood lactate by 22%. The active lignans, schisandrin and gomisin A, were shown to increase the activity of phase II detoxification enzymes in the liver, specifically glutathione S-transferase, by 1.8-fold.

*Withania somnifera* had been used in Ayurvedic medicine for over 3,000 years. The classical text *Charaka Samhita* (circa 600 BCE) describes it as a *rasayana* — a rejuvenative that “promotes longevity, improves cognition, and strengthens the body.” The modern understanding confirms that the withanolides (withaferin A, withanolide D) inhibit the NF-κB pathway, reducing the inflammatory response to cortisol by 40% in cell culture models.

*Eleutherococcus senticosus* — Siberian ginseng — was studied by Soviet researchers in the 1960s as a substitute for Panax ginseng, which was scarce and expensive. The eleutherosides (B, E, and E1) were shown to increase the number of T-lymphocytes by 30% in chronically stressed mice, and to reduce the incidence of respiratory infections in factory workers by 2.5 times over a six-month period.

The common thread: these plants were grown locally, harvested in season, and consumed in a form that preserved the full spectrum of active compounds. The modern extraction process — typically 70% ethanol maceration followed by spray drying at 180°C — destroys up to 60% of the heat-labile constituents.

## 4. The Protocol

### Cultivation Method: Seed to Dried Root

#### 4.1 *Withania somnifera* (Ashwagandha)

**Germination:** Scarify seeds by rubbing lightly between two sheets of 80-grit sandpaper for 10 seconds. Soak in water at 22°C for 12 hours. Sow in seed-starting mix at 1 cm depth. Maintain soil temperature at 25–28°C. Germination occurs in 7–14 days at >80% humidity.

**Transplanting:** Harden seedlings at 14 days after emergence. Transplant to garden bed at 45 cm spacing in rows 60 cm apart. Soil pH: 6.0–7.5. Full sun (6+ hours).

**Harvest:** Cut at soil level 120–140 days after emergence, when berries are orange-red. Wash roots thoroughly. Slice longitudinally into 1 cm strips.

**Drying:** Spread on mesh screens in a shed at 30–35°C with 40% relative humidity and airflow. Dry for 7–10 days until moisture content <10%. Root will snap cleanly when bent. Store in glass jars with oxygen absorbers at 15°C. Shelf life: 3 years.

**Active compound verification:** Withanolide content can be estimated by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) using a silica gel plate, chloroform:methanol (9:1) as mobile phase, and anisaldehyde-sulfuric acid spray. Withanolides appear as dark blue spots at Rf 0.4–0.6.

#### 4.2 *Rhodiola rosea* (Roseroot)

**Stratification:** Seeds require 4–6 weeks of cold stratification at 2–4°C. Mix seeds with moist sand in a sealed bag, store in a refrigerator. Check weekly for mold.

**Sowing:** After stratification, sow seeds on the surface of a sterile, sandy loam mix (1:1 sand:peat). Do not cover — light required for germination. Maintain at 18–22°C. Germination occurs in 21–35 days at 50–70% humidity.

**Transplanting:** Move seedlings to 10 cm pots after 4 true leaves. Grow in part shade (50% shade) for first year. In the second year, transplant to full sun at 30 cm spacing. Soil pH: 5.5–6.5. Requires excellent drainage — raised beds or rock gardens.

**Harvest:** Do not harvest in the first year. Harvest in the third year, in early spring before flowering, or in autumn after the first frost. Dig carefully to avoid breaking roots. Wash, slice into 0.5 cm discs.

**Drying:** Dry at 35°C max for 5–7 days. Do not exceed 40°C or rosavins degrade. Active compounds: rosavins and salidroside. Store in airtight containers away from light. Shelf life: 2 years.

#### 4.3 *Schisandra chinensis* (Schisandra)

**Stratification:** Seeds require 3 months of cold stratification. Soak for 24 hours, then mix with moist sand, store at 2–4°C. After stratification, warm stratify at 20°C for 2 weeks, then cold again for 2 weeks — this mimics natural conditions.

**Sowing:** Sow in seed trays at 1 cm depth. Maintain at 20–25°C. Germination is erratic — 30–60 days. Provide strong light.

**Transplanting:** Plant at 1.5 m spacing in a trellis system. Support with 2 m posts and wire. Prefers full sun but tolerates light shade. Soil pH: 6.0–7.0. Requires consistent moisture, especially during fruit development.

**Harvest:** Fruit production begins in years 3–4. Harvest when berries are bright red and soft (late summer). Strip clusters by hand. Dry whole in a food dehydrator at 35°C for 48 hours, or hang in bundles in a warm, dry shed for 2 weeks.

**Active compound verification:** Lignan content can be estimated by TLC using ethyl acetate:methanol:water (8:1:1). Schisandrin appears as a UV-absorbing spot at Rf 0.6.

#### 4.4 *Eleutherococcus senticosus* (Siberian Ginseng)

**Stratification:** Seeds require 4–5 months of cold stratification. Soak for 24 hours, mix with peat, store at 2–4°C. Check for mold every 2 weeks.

**Sowing:** Sow in deep pots (15 cm) at 1 cm depth. Maintain at 20°C. Germination occurs in 30–60 days. Grow for 1 year in pots before transplanting.

**Transplanting:** Plant at 1 m spacing in full sun or part shade. Soil pH: 5.5–7.0. This plant is a shrub that reaches 2–3 m. It is hardy to -40°C. Requires minimal care after establishment.

**Harvest:** Harvest roots in the fourth year, in autumn after leaf drop. Dig carefully to preserve the root system. Wash, slice into 1 cm pieces. Drying at 35°C for 7 days until brittle. Store in glass jars. Shelf life: 5 years.
