# Heat from Wood: Design of an Efficient Rocket Mass Heater for Heating a Small Building

## Abstract

Modern heating has made humans dependent on infrastructure: gas pipelines, circulation pumps, control electronics, and volatile energy prices. The Rocket Mass Heater (RMH) solves this problem using steel, clay, wood, and physics principles known for centuries. The design employs controlled biomass gasification, an insulated combustion riser, and energy storage in thermal mass, achieving extremely high efficiency with minimal fuel consumption.

This is not a decorative stove. This is a survival device.

# 1. Introduction

## The Hook

The average modern wood-burning stove expels a significant portion of energy up the chimney. This is visible to the naked eye: dense smoke is unburned fuel. Every wisp of gray smoke means lost hydrocarbons, carbon monoxide, and soot particles—energy that was cut, split, and dried only to be surrendered to the atmosphere.

The Rocket Mass Heater operates differently.

Instead of brutally burning wood, it induces pyrolysis and secondary combustion of wood gases in a chamber reaching temperatures exceeding 900–1100°C. The result is simple: more heat from less wood, less soot, longer energy release, and minimal chimney draft.

In a world dependent on natural gas and control electronics, the RMH remains a brutally simple thermodynamic machine.

# 2. Background

## The Lost History

The foundations of the rocket stove were laid not in the laboratories of energy corporations, but in conditions of fuel scarcity. In the 1970s, engineers working on high-efficiency stoves for developing regions discovered that the appropriate geometry of the combustion chamber radically increased the efficiency of firewood.

Later, Ianto Evans and Larry Winiarski developed the concept of the Rocket Mass Heater—a system combining high-temperature combustion with thermal mass made of clay, brick, and sand.

Our ancestors understood one thing that modernity has abandoned: heat must be stored, not merely produced.
